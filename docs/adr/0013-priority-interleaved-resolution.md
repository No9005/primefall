# Priority Interleaved Resolution

Primefall resolves each round through priority-interleaved action queues. During planning, each Hero receives an ordered queue of actions; during Resolution, the engine processes one action from every non-exhausted Hero’s queue per Tick.

For example, if one Hero plans Move, Attack, Block and its opponent plans Attack, Move, Block, Tick 1 resolves the first Hero’s Move and the opponent’s Attack; Tick 2 resolves the first Hero’s Attack and the opponent’s Move; Tick 3 resolves both Blocks.

Actions are validated at execution time. An action that has become illegal—because its target is exhausted, no longer visible, or otherwise unavailable—fails without affecting later actions. Simultaneous attacks both resolve and can both cause damage. Heroes that move do not hinder each other, but a Hero cannot walk through a Hero that stays where it is. A Move (up to 2 steps towards its destination) is walked step by step in the replay. While walking, a Hero may pass over a tile where another Hero is at that moment if that other Hero also moves in this Tick (it leaves, arrives or crosses), so Heroes can follow each other in a corridor, cross, or swap tiles, and two Heroes may be on one tile in the middle of the Tick. A Hero that stays counts as an obstacle: a Hero that does not move, whose own Move ends where it started, that has finished its Move, that is on a Wait or another action that is not a Move, or that is Exhausted and still on the board; a walk stops in front of it. Only the tile where the Tick's movement ends must be free: no two Heroes may be on the same tile then. If planned end tiles collide (two Heroes ending on one tile, or a Hero ending on the tile of a Hero that stays), the Heroes involved stop one step earlier on their own path, all at once. This is repeated until nothing changes, because a shortened walk may make another walk collide and a Hero that cannot move at all stays on its start tile and becomes an obstacle; so the result does not depend on the order of the Heroes. Walls, Wall corners and Full Cover still block a Move. The rule is decided for all Heroes together, so it is the same for allies and enemies, for the player and the AI, and does not favour a Squad. A `MoveBlocked` event is emitted only when a Move ends short of its destination (by a Wall, a Wall corner or a Hero); it says where the Hero stopped. Knockback keeps its own rule and still stops before another Hero.

A Declared Target (the enemy Hero an Attack names in planning) is validated at execution time, including visibility: in the Tick the Attack fires it is checked, in this order, for being visible to the attacker's Squad, not Exhausted, within Range, in Line of Sight and not behind Full Cover. The first failing check makes the Attack a Miss with that reason; it does nothing and later actions still run.

A Block takes effect immediately in its Tick and can absorb damage from an attack resolved in that same Tick. This makes action order a core tactical decision: moving into cover before attacking, attacking before an enemy repositions, or blocking before an expected shot all produce different outcomes.

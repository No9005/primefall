# Priority Interleaved Resolution

Primefall resolves each round through priority-interleaved action queues. During planning, each Hero receives an ordered queue of actions; during Resolution, the engine processes one action from every non-exhausted Hero’s queue per Tick.

For example, if one Hero plans Move, Attack, Block and its opponent plans Attack, Move, Block, Tick 1 resolves the first Hero’s Move and the opponent’s Attack; Tick 2 resolves the first Hero’s Attack and the opponent’s Move; Tick 3 resolves both Blocks.

Actions are validated at execution time. An action that has become illegal—because its target is exhausted, no longer visible, or otherwise unavailable—fails without affecting later actions. Simultaneous attacks both resolve and can both cause damage. Heroes may walk through each other: a Move (up to 2 steps towards its destination) passes tiles occupied by other Heroes, allies, enemies and Exhausted Heroes alike. Walls, Wall corners and Full Cover still block it. The only thing not allowed is two Heroes standing on the same tile when a Move ends. All Heroes take step n before any takes step n+1, and a Move must end on a tile that no other Hero stands on at the end of that step; otherwise the Hero stops on the last free tile of its walk (it stays where it is if every tile of its walk is occupied). If two Heroes attempt to end a Move on the same free tile, both stop on the last free tile of their own walk before it: nobody gets the tile. The rule is decided for all Heroes together, so it is the same for allies and enemies, for the player and the AI, and does not favour a Squad. Only a Move that could not reach its destination emits a `MoveBlocked` event, which says where the Hero stopped. Knockback keeps its own rule and still stops before another Hero.

A Block takes effect immediately in its Tick and can absorb damage from an attack resolved in that same Tick. This makes action order a core tactical decision: moving into cover before attacking, attacking before an enemy repositions, or blocking before an expected shot all produce different outcomes.
